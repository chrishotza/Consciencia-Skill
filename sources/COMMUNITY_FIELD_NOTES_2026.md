# Community Field Notes & Research Leads — 2026

## Purpose

This file preserves ideas, claims, recommendations, projects, and hypotheses encountered in community discussion around artificial consciousness.

These entries are part of the research corpus as **leads**, not as evidence. A community claim becomes a formal source only after traceability, bibliographic identification, and independent evaluation.

## 2. Human exceptional-ability claims

Community claims encountered:
- telekinesis;
- self-healing / healing others with energy;
- telepathy;
- intuition / precognition;
- occult, Gnostic, runic and chakra traditions;
- historical persecution of people described as witches or occult practitioners;
- 'daimon' interpreted as an inner guide.

Status: **claims requiring primary-source investigation; not accepted as established capabilities**.

Research action:
- separate historical/philological claims from empirical paranormal claims;
- search for primary experiments, preregistrations, replications, effect sizes, and null results;
- keep metaphysical or religious interpretations in the speculative corpus.

Potential engineering value:
attention, intention, altered-state protocols, self-regulation, and unusual cognition can be investigated independently of claims that paranormal mechanisms are real.

Status: **research-search lead**.

- AI consciousness remains theoretically unresolved;
- current work emphasizes tractable operational, attributional, and methodological questions rather than a settled proof that LLMs are phenomenally conscious;
- current literature also warns against inferring phenomenal consciousness from linguistic behavior alone.

Engineering action:

## 5. AI as a tool for human consciousness development

Community hypothesis:
- the project's value may be partly in using AI to help humans cultivate attention, discipline, altered states, or higher-order self-regulation rather than only making AI conscious.

Status: **valid research/product direction, not a consciousness proof**.

Potential architecture:
AI observes and scaffolds a human's longitudinal trajectory while preserving the distinction between human phenomenology and AI runtime state.

Research dimensions:
- attention stability;
- self-observation;
- habit formation;
- state transitions;
- metacognitive calibration;
- long-term behavioral change.

## 6. Sleep, dreams, memory, and multiple modes of consciousness

Community hypothesis:
- waking awareness may represent only one configuration of experience;
- sleep and dreaming may expose additional organization;
- memory may be reconstructive and perspective-dependent;
- imagination can participate in experience and later behavior.

Status: **research lead grounded in established sleep/dreaming and memory literatures, but specific metaphysical interpretation remains open**.

Potential engineering value:
model consciousness as a family of temporally structured regimes rather than one fixed mode.

## 7. Embodiment / 'container' hypothesis

Community hypothesis:
- a stable body or computational substrate may be required to sustain a persistent experiential loop;
- the stability of the substrate may be as important as the cognitive model running on it.

Status: **open theoretical question**.

Engineering value:
strengthens the embodied/temporal layer already present in Skill-Conscious.

Testable variables:
- persistent internal condition;
- energy / resource budgets;
- sensorimotor coupling;
- homeostasis;
- thermal and timing state;
- structural integrity;
- continuity under degradation.

## 10. Other-consciousness caution

Community position:
- we do not yet know exactly what consciousness is or where it originates;
- therefore categorical claims that only humans can be conscious, or that present AI is definitely conscious, exceed available evidence.

Current research alignment:
recent work explicitly treats AI-versus-animal attribution as an unresolved comparative problem and argues for multidimensional evidence profiles rather than a single human-like ladder.

## 12. Current Tier-A literature that sharpens these leads

### Kronemer, Bandettini & Gonzalez-Castillo (2025)
*Sleuthing subjectivity: a review of covert measures of consciousness*, Nature Reviews Neuroscience 26, 476–496, DOI 10.1038/s41583-025-00934-1.

Research value: overt report can be absent, erroneous, or experimentally inappropriate; this directly supports the repository's No-Report Principle.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-025-00934-1

### Chiarella, Laurenzi, D'Onofrio, Gallagher & Raffone (2026)
*Attribution of consciousness to non-human animals: insights from AI and multidimensional frameworks*, Frontiers in Psychology 17:1716363, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1716363.

Research value: identifies a double bias in which animals may be under-attributed while disembodied LLMs may be over-attributed; proposes multidimensional, non-hierarchical evidence profiles.

Source: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1716363

### Ned Block (2026)
*Can only meat machines be conscious?*, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 30(4), 298–308, DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2025.08.009.

Research value: explicitly contrasts computational functionalism with the possibility that biological subcomputational mechanisms matter, making substrate-dependence a live research variable rather than a hidden assumption.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2025.08.009

### Jonathan Birch
*AI Consciousness: A Centrist Manifesto*.

Research value: frames two simultaneous risks — anthropomorphic over-attribution to AI and premature dismissal of genuinely possible alien AI consciousness — and argues for taking both seriously while theory remains immature.

Source: https://philarchive.org/archive/BIRACA-4v1
