# David Hume — consciousness, self, continuity, and causation

## Primary source

**David Hume, _A Treatise of Human Nature_ (1739–1740), Book I, Part IV, Section VI, “Of personal identity.”**

Primary text:
https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/6

Related Appendix discussion:
https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/full

## Why this source belongs in the consciousness library

Hume directly addresses the question of the **self as it appears in thought and consciousness**. He argues that we do not encounter a single, constant impression corresponding to a simple enduring self; instead, thought presents a succession or bundle of perceptions connected by relations among them.

This is directly relevant to Skill-Conscious because the repository also treats identity as a continuity relation rather than as an unchanging substance.

Hume also matters because his treatment of causation is unusually useful as an adversarial constraint: he distinguishes observed regularities and mental association from any direct perception of necessary connection. That forces the engineering program to say exactly what it means when it calls a self-model change “causal.”

## Source-derived claims

### 1. No directly observed simple self

Hume challenges the claim that we have an immediately given, simple and perfectly identical self. He argues that the self is not one invariant impression.

### 2. The mind is a succession / bundle of perceptions

For the purposes of thought and imagination, Hume describes personal identity in terms of a succession or bundle of perceptions rather than a separately observed substance.

### 3. Connections matter

Perceptions are connected through principles of association, including resemblance, contiguity, and causation. The identity attributed to the mind is therefore not simply a list; the organization of relations between successive contents matters.

### 4. Hume himself leaves a problem open

In the Appendix to the *Treatise*, Hume acknowledges that his account of the principle connecting successive perceptions is defective. This is important: the source should be used as a pressure test, not treated as a finished theory of consciousness.

### 5. Consciousness is explicitly in the discussion

Hume's analysis is not merely a theory of memory storage. He discusses claims about being “intimately conscious” of the self and later addresses the relation between personal identity and thought or consciousness.

## What we extract — and what we do not

We **do extract**:

- identity as relational continuity rather than assumed substance;
- the possibility that a stable self is a construction over changing states;
- the importance of transitions and associations between successive states;
- skepticism about treating causal necessity as directly observed.

We **do not extract**:

- the claim that the bundle theory has already solved consciousness;
- the claim that subjective experience is reducible to a list of perceptions;
- the claim that persistent architecture is unnecessary merely because a bundle description is possible.

## Engineering translation

Hume becomes an **adversarial lens** for Skill-Conscious:

~~~text
CHANGING STATES
      ↓
RELATIONS / ASSOCIATIONS
      ↓
CONTINUITY OF IDENTITY
      ↓
SELF-MODEL
      ↓
FUTURE TRAJECTORY
~~~

The critical question becomes:

> **Does a persistent self-model add causal functionality that cannot be recovered from the current bundle of perceptions alone?**

That is a testable engineering question.

## Proposed Hume adversarial test

Hold the present input and current self-state constant.

Compare:

1. **Persistent-self condition** — prior information is carried forward into a persistent self-model and allowed to influence trajectory selection.
2. **Bundle-only condition** — the same prior information remains available only as a record of perceptions, without a privileged persistent self-model affecting trajectory weights.

Then test whether:

- trajectory selection diverges;
- the divergence is reversible under intervention;
- the difference survives restart;
- the effect can be reproduced by giving the bundle-only condition an equally expressive relational mechanism.

A positive result would not prove consciousness. It would show that the architecture has a functional property not captured by the chosen bundle-only baseline.

A null result would be valuable too: it would push the project toward a more Humean relational formulation.

## Causal interpretation

For Skill-Conscious, “causal” should remain operational:

> intervene on a defined internal variable, keep the relevant comparison conditions fixed, and measure whether downstream state or trajectory changes.

This is narrower than a metaphysical proof of necessary connection.
