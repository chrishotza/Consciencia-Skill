# Experience Geometry

Skill-Conscious now exposes an operational geometry for comparing integrated runtime states.

The design is inspired by a methodological point in consciousness research: avoid assuming that consciousness is a single scalar level and instead compare states through multidimensional distances. Enzo Tagliazucchi describes representations in terms of spaces and networks in which different conscious states can be compared by their distances rather than against an absolute “normal consciousness” reference.

## Representation

The runtime projects a state into bounded dimensions:

- valence
- coherence
- self-dissonance
- salience
- metacognitive uncertainty
- self-observation error
- prediction error
- self-relevance
- field coherence
- dynamic synchrony
- dynamic metastability
- dynamic complexity
- dynamic repertoire

The projection is an engineering state vector. It is not a consciousness score.

## Distance

For two states A and B:

```text
distance(A, B) =
    sqrt(mean((A_i - B_i)^2))
```

The result is bounded to [0, 1].

The runtime persists transitions with:

```text
previous_state
    ↓
experience_distance
    ↓
changed_dimensions
    ↓
current_state
```

This creates a machine-readable trajectory through an operational experience space.

## Why this matters

This makes several future experiments possible without assuming phenomenal consciousness:

- detect stable neighborhoods and recurring experiential states;
- measure abrupt versus gradual transitions;
- compare interventions by their location in experience space;
- search for attractors in the experience manifold;
- compare model-generated descriptions with runtime-derived state geometry;
- test whether apparently different internal states converge on similar operational experience states.

The architecture deliberately does not call one state “more conscious” than another. That would impose a scalar theory that the experimental literature does not justify.
